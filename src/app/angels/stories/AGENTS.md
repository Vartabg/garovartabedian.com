# Visual story direction

These are visual stories: let artwork, scale, light, and the reader's movement carry the emotional turn. Keep prose restrained and rooted in the passage.

- Generate generously for distinct narrative moments. Give arrival, encounter, and aftermath their own compositions. Do not substitute repeated crops of one picture for new story art.
- Use paired images together when their registration is the point of one interaction; do not repeat that same pair in separate narrative beats.
- Vary interaction and pacing across the collection. Dothan's direct image comparison, Bethel's exploration of scale, and Peter's moving light reveal serve different feelings. A new story needs its own reason for its technique.
- Put touch interaction on the artwork itself. Prefer meaningful image manipulation over decorative props; the constructed gate/shutter effect was explicitly rejected.
- Preserve ordinary vertical scrolling and browser pinch zoom. Provide keyboard and button/range equivalents, cancellation handling, reduced motion, and a complete no-JavaScript reading.
- Clearly label all generated depictions as artistic interpretations, not accurate historical representations. Keep quotations and full passages exact; do not infer an angelic rank from artwork.
- Verify gestures with real touch input, inspect the actual rendered art on desktop and narrow screens, and check each narrative image loads without repeated sources.

See `docs/angels-stories-collection.md` and `docs/angels-story-touch.md` for the creative standard, image prompts, and implementation decisions.
