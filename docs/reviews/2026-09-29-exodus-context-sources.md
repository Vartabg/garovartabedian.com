# Exodus context: source trail and review questions

Prepared 2026-09-29 for the [Exodus book briefing](../../public/scriptures/books/EXO.json). This is an agent check of the hosted text, source descriptions, and editorial limits. It records **no human or specialist approval**. Chapter authors should read each hosted chapter and its neighbors; a book-level source cannot certify every local inference.

## Hosted editions and measurements

The English grounding for this batch is the site's [World English Bible Exodus](../../public/scriptures/text/web/EXO.json), identified in the [public Scripture source notes](../../public/scriptures/SOURCES.md). All 40 chapters and 1,213 nonempty verse slots were read. Concatenating verse strings and splitting on whitespace yields **30,520 English words**. Under the same method, Genesis has 35,623, Numbers 30,770, Deuteronomy 26,803, and Leviticus 23,181. The inherited briefing called Exodus second-longest while itself listing a larger count for Numbers; the reader briefing now omits the unhelpful rank. These numbers describe hosted English files, not ancient Hebrew composition.

The WEB file contains 397 case-sensitive occurrences of `Yahweh`, seven of `Let my people go` (5:1; 7:16; 8:1; 8:20; 9:1; 9:13; 10:3), and 14 of `as Yahweh commanded Moses` in chapters 39–40 (seven in each chapter). Case, inflection, and translation choice change such counts. The [hosted Open Scriptures Hebrew Bible layer](../../public/scriptures/original/SOURCES.md) was checked at the cited lexical verses. It is a pinned, pointed Westminster Leningrad Codex text, with lemma and morphology data; it is a medieval witness and cannot by itself date the book's formation.

The site also hosts [Brenton's English Septuagint Exodus](../../public/scriptures/text/brenton/EXO.json). Direct hosted comparison shows WEB 37:1 begins the ark while Brenton 37:1 begins the curtains; WEB 38:1 begins the burnt-offering altar while Brenton 38:1 begins the ark. In chapters 37, 38, and 39, the WEB files have 29, 31, and 43 verse slots; Brenton has 21, 27, and 23. Brenton also has empty slots in parts of the construction account. The two English editions cannot safely share verse-level content references across chapters 35–39. This comparison does **not** establish the earliest Greek or Hebrew wording. [Dayfani's research abstract](https://aabner.org/ojs/index.php/beabs/article/view/1031) identifies several distinct ancient forms of the construction account and frames reconstruction of 4Q22 as a proposal.

## Source access and corrections

The USCCB introduction and chapter notes, Hendel chapter, BAS pages, Farber article, and hosted files supplied the statements identified above. Dayfani was checked at the published article **abstract**; Hoffmeier at the publisher **abstract**; Lexham at an accessible **excerpt**. These limited pages are labeled narrowly in the source catalog. The prior Jewish Encyclopedia URL did not resolve reliably and its broad description was replaced with directly checked sources. The general IAA archive remains background for scroll access, not proof of a particular variant.

Several inherited claims were narrowed or removed: Exodus's false word-count rank; “every recoverable form ... Hebrew”; an exact two-Pharaoh claim; Miriam's basket-watcher identification as an explicit statement of 2:4; Jethro “inventing” a court system; the artisans as the Bible's first Spirit-filled people; a claim of unanimous premodern authorship; a confident ancient-audience reaction to tabernacle repetition; and the claim that textual differences are small. The briefing now distinguishes narrated chronology, textual witnesses, modern English translations, and proposals for composition. The named historical positions are not a vote-count of current scholars.

## Independent review queue

1. Ask a Hebrew specialist to check the displayed Hebrew forms, transliterations, the three hardening roots, *‘avad*, *ehyeh*, *pesach*, *mishkan*, and *kavod* against a critical text and lexicon. The site's OSHB word data is a useful locator, not a replacement for lexical judgment.
2. Review Exodus 4:24–26 with its exact WEB wording: WEB explicitly names Moses at 4:24, while the Hebrew pronoun structure and “bridegroom of blood” leave other details difficult. [USCCB's chapter note](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/exodus/4) favors neglected circumcision as the background; do not label it the text's explicit explanation.
3. At Exodus 6:3, keep the direction straight: **God was not known to the ancestors by this name**. Compare Genesis's use of YHWH and the [USCCB contextual note](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/exodus/6) before presenting first-disclosure or experienced-meaning readings.
4. At Exodus 12, 14–15, 21–23, and 32, check that plague deaths, drowning, enslavement, sale and beating of servants, threatened violence, and the Levite killings are described directly and proportionately. Agent review is not a named human ethics review.
5. At Exodus 33–34, retain the face/visibility tension and distinguish the commands “write these words” (34:27), the WEB pronoun “He wrote” (34:28), and God's promise to write on the replacement tablets (34:1). Do not resolve the 34:28 subject merely by importing an interpretation.
6. For every construction guide, verify the selected edition and the actual verse contents. Brenton 35–39 does not follow WEB's object order; identical chapter-and-verse labels can point to different objects. Keep translation comparisons narrower than claims about original Greek or Hebrew priority.

The specialist questions above remain a queue for possible human review. The completed agent chapter review is recorded below.
