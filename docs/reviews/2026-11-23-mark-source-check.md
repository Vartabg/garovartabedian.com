# Mark chapter guides: source check, second pass, 2026-11-23

**Status: applied on 2026-11-23.** Garo ruled on six calls that day, and then
on the sweep below. This file records the changes to the guides. The labels
on the stamp are in the guides' own provenance.

It follows `2026-11-21-mark-source-check.md`, which left 6 claims open, 15
backed in part and 10 marked as readings.

## The rule this pass works to

`scripts/scriptures/GUIDE_RUBRIC_V3.md` now says: a language claim, or a
claim about history, law, custom or geography that the hosted text does not
supply, is left out of a draft. A reviewer may keep one only when the book briefing
has it or a source check record names what it rests on. Where one reading is
given alone, the sentence says it is a reading.

## How

The same method as the first pass. Word and cross-reference claims were run
against the hosted text. For the rest a helper fetched a named page and
quoted it. Where nothing read backs a claim, the claim is cut back to what
the hosted text says.

## Limits of this pass

- The sources are old reference works (Thayer's, Strong's, Whiston's Josephus), the Mishnah in Sefaria's English, and Wikipedia. They show a claim is standard; they are not current scholarship.
- Josephus, War 5.5.8 was read word for word only after the chapter 11 sentence was trimmed; it says soldiers watched the cloisters "on the Jewish festivals". The sentence was left trimmed.
- James Barr's 1988 article is known from summaries and a citation only.
- The helpers' quotations were not re-read by the lead in this pass.
- The sweep was one reader's pass with a stated bar ("a careful editor would ask: says who?"). It did not list readings, and a claim below that bar may remain.
- Seven kept claims are marked not checked. Each is worded as someone's view or is a general characterisation.
- Three places lean on hosted verses that critical editions leave out or shorten (Mark 9:29 "and fasting", Mark 10:29 "wife", Luke 23:17). The hosted text is the rubric's authority, so they stand.
