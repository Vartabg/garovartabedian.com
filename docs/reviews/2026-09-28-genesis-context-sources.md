# Genesis context: source trail and review questions

Prepared 2026-09-28 for the [Genesis book briefing](../../public/scriptures/books/GEN.json). This is an agent source and text check, **not a record of human approval**. The briefing is book-level grounding for a 50-chapter guide pass; chapter authors still need to read each hosted chapter and its neighbors before writing local context.

## Editions checked here

The default English chapter text is this site's [World English Bible](../../public/scriptures/text/web/GEN.json), identified in the public [Scripture source notes](../../public/scriptures/SOURCES.md). It has 50 chapters, 1,533 nonempty verse slots, and 35,623 words when whitespace-separated strings in all verse slots are counted. “Word count” here describes that English file, not a Hebrew word count. The chapter and verse arrays were checked directly, as were all single-verse references in the briefing.

The site also hosts [Brenton's English Septuagint](../../public/scriptures/text/brenton/GEN.json). Direct comparison of these hosted English texts finds 130 years at Seth's birth in WEB Genesis 5:3 against 230 in Brenton, and WEB's Shelah against Brenton's Cainan in 11:12. This comparison establishes what readers see here; it does not decide which ancient witness preserves an earlier number or name. The [original-language source notes](../../public/scriptures/original/SOURCES.md) identify the pinned Open Scriptures Hebrew Bible/Westminster Leningrad Codex layer used for Hebrew words. Its pointed Masoretic text is a surviving witness, not a manuscript from Genesis's composition.

## Independent review queue

1. Have a Hebrew Bible specialist check the transcription and translation of *toledot*, *adam/adamah*, *ish/ishah*, Isaac's laughter, and the Aramaic/Hebrew pair at 31:47 against a critical text. The pinned word layer helps locate forms but does not decide disputed vocalization or etymology.
2. Review the composition paragraph for proportion. The anonymous narrator, Moses tradition, sixth/fifth-century editorial proposal, and alternate growth models should remain distinguishable. Neither an exact year nor an individual editor is recoverable from these sources.
3. If a guide makes a historical claim about the ancestors, compare it with critical scholarship on the setting and limits of external evidence. Do not turn a narrative place, costume, or name into a demonstrated date.
4. If a guide compares WEB with Brenton, check that chapter's actual hosted wording and numbering. Keep Genesis 5 and 11 genealogical differences visible and avoid silently blending names or ages.
5. Have a named human reviewer assess the moral and theological proportion of notes on Hagar, enslavement, sexual violence, the flood, and the sale of Joseph before describing the batch as human reviewed. No such review is recorded here.

For guide writing, use the briefing as orientation and the hosted WEB chapter as the source of quotations. Cite the book sources only when they support the specific claim in the chapter. An agent check of references and URLs is useful evidence of consistency; it is not a substitute for specialist or human editorial judgment.

## Completed chapter pass

All 50 chapters now have chapter-specific outlines, summaries, questions, glossary notes, and before/here/after reading context. The batch contains 381 questions and 150 complete WEB verse quotations. The before and after references connect each chapter to its neighbors without inventing a chapter before Genesis 1 or after Genesis 50.

Two editorial agents drafted chapters 1–25 and 26–50. A separate research agent independently reviewed chapters 1–25, and the first-half author independently reviewed chapters 26–50. Each independent reviewer read the hosted WEB chapter text, neighboring transitions, final book briefing, rubric, and exact prepared prompt and grounding bundle. Review included quotation matching, bounded references, edition differences, and difficult passages involving violence, enslavement, deception, and family power. The root editor applied the reviewers' corrections, and the respective independent reviewer rechecked the saved text before clearance.

Corrections included identifying Brenton as an English Septuagint translation; removing editorial instructions from reader-facing prose; removing an unnecessary Jubilees comparison from Genesis 27; following WEB's wording in Genesis 31; and avoiding an unsupported claim that Genesis 47's levy lasts permanently. Both independent passes reported no remaining substantive finding. Fresh grounding packets matched all 50 prepared targets, and all 150 complete quotations matched the hosted WEB text.

The review stamp records `agent-reviewed`, the WEB source edition, rubric `guide-v2`, and review-bound provenance. “Codex agents (model not measured)” is a reviewer attribution, not a claimed API generation measurement. The generation timestamp records this binding step. These are agent editorial checks; no human or specialist sign-off is recorded.

The reader now names the edition used by the context references. A reader viewing Brenton can therefore see that a context link opens WEB, the edition checked for this batch.
