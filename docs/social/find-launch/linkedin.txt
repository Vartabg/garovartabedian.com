I’ve rebuilt Find on garovartabedian.com to help readers explore connections between Scripture and early Christian writings—and stay close to the actual text.

Search a phrase. Open the passage in its named edition. Follow a source note, compare the wording, and return to the place you were reading.

The part I’m especially interested in sharing is how I’ve used Jev from TypeSafe AI.

Search retrieves the passages first. When a reader chooses a relevance check or a supported passage comparison, Jev evaluates the selected text and its surrounding context through narrowly defined questions: Does this passage address the question? Is the connection close wording, paraphrase, or only a shared theme?

The original passages stay visible. AI assessments are labeled Unreviewed, and model uncertainty stays visible too. Similarity alone does not establish historical borrowing, agreement, or proof.

Around that, I’ve built a calmer reading experience: clear paragraphs, adjustable typography and page themes, edition-aware links, and source notes that bring you back to your place.

Visitors can try the optional AI checks without their own API key, with a daily browser allowance and shared service limits. Ordinary reading and word search don’t call Jev.

This is an experimental research aid. Independent evaluation of its relevance judgments is still in progress.
