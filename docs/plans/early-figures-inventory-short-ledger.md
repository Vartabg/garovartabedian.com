Boundary: developed comparisons, extended metaphors, meaningful images and the author's interpretive use of scriptural images/typology qualify. Routine idioms, unelaborated moral instructions and literal narrative without interpretive imaging normally do not. This involves judgment; no claim of an objectively exhaustive metaphor inventory is made. `P` = passage present in an existing selection, even if inside a longer differently titled entry; `T` = qualifying sequence partly present, with the unselected part stated; `M` = missing as a selected passage, and `id: —` means no existing selection ID, not a proposed ID. Several distinct candidates can occur in one chapter. `A` = letter/teaching's author or collective voice; `Q` = A quotes scripture or a saying; `I` = A's interpretation; `prayer` denotes the written prayer, not an asserted historical speaker.

`G+` lists direct existing `related`/`relatedAlso` Gospel links of the relevant selected ID at the snapshot. `G?` lists possible **missing** links for editorial assessment, not claims of dependence or exact identity; it gives the target ID and a short difference. A dash means no link proposed in this review, not proof no possible comparison exists. The target may exist even when its link from a selection does not. Comparisons never transfer the letter's speaker to the Gospel or vice versa.

## Handoff priorities and limitations

1. **Passages missing at this snapshot:** examples include 1 Clement 2:8, 3:1, 5:2–6, 10:4–6, 14:5, 16:3 and 15, 17:2 and 6, 18:5 and 14, 21:2–3, 29:2–3, 39:3–9, 51:3–4 and 57:3–7; Barnabas 4:4–11, 5:2 and 10, 9:6–7, 14:1–5 and 15:3–8; Diognetus 2:2–9 and 11:4. Rows above are the full chapter-by-chapter list; these examples are not a substitute for it.
2. **Partial-sequence decisions:** Barnabas 7:3–5, 9:1–3 and 12:1 and Diognetus 5:10–17 surround existing narrower selections. Decide whether to extend or justify each boundary. Long legacy selections already cover Didache 1–6 and 16, 1 Clement 20 and 46, and Barnabas 6 and 10; do not create duplicates just because titles omit a particular figure.
3. **Links are separate from selection coverage.** In particular, the already selected Didache 6:2 yoke has no direct `synoptic-yoke` relation at the captured snapshot; the additional G? links are optional editorial comparisons and several deliberately contrast different speakers or interpretations. Never imply common authorship or literary dependence from image overlap.
4. **Text and attribution:** preserve authors' quotations and interpretations as such. For example, 1 Clement 13 attributes words to Jesus; 1 Clement 16 quotes Isaiah; Barnabas 9 reads numbers typologically; Diognetus 11–12 should be attributed to the closing section's author without asserting shared authorship. Source transcription quality needs separate edition verification: hosted Didache 1:3 duplicates words; chapter 16:1 does too. Differences between legacy and hosted editions cannot be normalized silently. Sensitive polemics and ancient claims about animal biology require clear attribution.
5. This inventory is a reasoned reading, not proof that all readers would classify every metaphor alike or a claim that every proposed Gospel relationship should be added. It does not verify textual fidelity against a scanned critical or historical edition, test rendered links, or audit Acts of Thomas. Reconcile this snapshot's IDs and links against the final files before acting, because other work was editing them concurrently.
