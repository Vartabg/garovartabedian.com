# Other Parables-room writings: source and coverage audit

Baseline master 265eab56958b77db0a23a3ab65829fc5ad0f0e7b, the merged PR #230 tree; `public/jesus/{parables,synoptic-figures,john-figures,thomas-parables,james-parables,early-figures}.json`. No website edits. The previous full audit covered Didache, 1 Clement, Barnabas, Diognetus and Acts of Thomas; they are not re-audited here. Writings outside canon are historical documents and speakers are internal literary attributions. No claim that a loose comparison establishes dependence.

Scope actually checked: all 114 Mattison Thomas sayings against all `thomas` links and three standalone Thomas entries; all 12 James sections against 13 entries; 20 chapters of 2 Clement; all 18 Philip and 16 Truth sections; 51 James-translation chapters of Epistula Apostolorum; Hermas's 30 source sections, with source sections already fully included treated as covered and other sections read. For the four Gospels a broad lexical scan of uncovered WEB verses across all 89 chapters identified and was reviewed for candidates; it is not a line-by-line or exhaustive metaphor audit. Theophany: checked the cited fragment and searched book 4; whole Theophany has not been audited. Automated keyword screening can miss figures expressed using other words. This was a single-reviewer audit, not an independent second review; no claim of completeness.

Inclusion rule: developed comparisons, parables, unusual metaphors with definite application, and direct early parallels to existing figures. Routine idioms and merely topical similarities are optional. A single selected paragraph may contain several figures; avoid splitting or duplicating one merely because its title differs. References and source numbering below follow the current hosted editions; Coptic page numbers are physical manuscript pages, not modern verse numbers.

## New candidates: Gospel of Thomas

* Saying 7: lion and human, paradoxical animal image; Jesus in the text. Meaning disputed; translation and potential emendations require comparison, not an invented conclusion.
* 60: Samaritan carrying a lamb, object lesson and different application; no asserted Good Samaritan connection. Jesus and disciples speak.
* 102: dog in feeding trough preventing cattle eating; Jesus's comparison concerning Pharisees. Partial thematic relationship to Matthew 23:13/Luke 11:52 and Thomas 39, not the same story.
* 32: hilltop city, compare Matthew 5:14, currently omitted. 24: light within person, partial topical comparison Matthew 5:14–16 and John 8:12; not identical.
* 39: keys of knowledge and hindering entry, compare Luke 11:52 and Matthew 23:13; its serpents/doves compare Matthew 10:16. The parallels are distinct parts, not one identical account.
* 40: unrooted vine pulled up, compare Matthew 15:13 and Philip Uprooting Evil paragraph 5 (a quotation of that Gospel saying); interpretive contexts differ.
* 71: destroying a house, compare John 2:19–21 and Gospel-trial report Matthew 26:61/Mark 14:58, with wording and speaker-context differences; Thomas is fragmentary and does not say three days.
* 73: plentiful harvest and few workers, compare Matthew 9:37–38 and Luke 10:2.
* 76 second half: treasure safe from destructive creatures; existing pearl comparison already displays full saying but does not connect that half to Matthew 6:19–21/Luke 12:33–34. Do not add duplicate standalone as if absent.
* 78: reed shaken by wind, compare Matthew 11:7–8/Luke 7:24–25; existing fine-clothing Gospel entry starts at Matt 11:8/Luke 7:25. Enlarge accounts if showing this connection.
* 86: foxes' dens and birds' nests, compare Matthew 8:20/Luke 9:58.
* 89: inside/outside cup, compare Matthew 23:25–26/Luke 11:39–41; wording and rhetorical application differ.
* 90: easy yoke, compare Matthew 11:28–30, already in the room without Thomas. 93: holy things/dogs and pearls/swine, compare Matthew 7:6; ending is damaged. 106: two made one and a moving mountain, partial overlaps 2 Clement 12:2–6/Thomas 22 and Matthew 17:20/21:21, Mark 11:23, Luke 17:6, without identical framing.
* Additional image or context comparisons: 5–6 hidden/revealed (Mark 4:22/Luke 8:17); 10 fire (Luke 12:49); 13 spring and intoxication (John 4:13–14, James Being Filled 1, thematic only); 28 drunkenness/blindness (James Being Filled and John 9, thematic); 3 kingdom within/outside (Luke 17:20–21, not identical); 48 mountain (Mark 11:23, different conditions); 91 reading sky (Luke 12:54–56, Matthew 16:2b–3, whose longer wording has manuscript uncertainty). Sayings 70 and 77 contain further figurative images without an obvious direct comparison; classify as standalone only if the same broad boundary is applied. Source: https://www.gospels.net/thomas/.

## 2 Clement

* 6:1–5: two masters followed by two ages as enemies, compare Matthew 6:24/Luke 16:13 for first image; speaker is the homilist quoting the Lord then commenting.
* 8:4–6: trust in small/great things, quotation attributed to the Lord and explained differently; compare Luke 16:10–12 in already hosted unjust steward. It is separate from the potter at 8:1–3.
* 9:3–8: flesh as temple, God as physician; partial image comparisons only to John 2:19–21 and Matthew 9:12–13/Mark 2:17/Luke 5:31–32; subject and application differ.
* 14:1: author quotes 'house ... den of robbers' to contrast the church of life; compare Matthew 21:13/Mark 11:17/Luke 19:46 as shared scriptural quotation, not a retold parable.
* Other narrower candidates: 1:6 darkness/cloud and regaining sight; 2:1–3 barren woman, author explicitly interprets Isa 54:1; 3:4–5 lips and heart, cites Isaiah 29:13 (Gospel Matthew 15:7–9/Mark 7:6–7 already displayed); 16:3 furnace/day, 20:2–4 cultivation/slow harvest. Existing 5:2–4, 7:1–5, 8:1–3, 11:2–5, 12:2–6 are already included. Source https://ccel.org/ccel/lightfoot/fathers/fathers.ii.ii.html.

## Gospel of Philip

* Coptic 63, Becoming Christians paragraphs 8–9: glass and pottery vessels; a donkey turning a millstone while getting nowhere. The narrator speaks, not a saying attributed to Jesus. Standalone comparisons, not Gospel parables. Compare 2 Clement 8 potter / Gospel of Truth jars only at the image level.
* Coptic 79–80, Spiritual Growth paragraph 1: faith, hope, love and knowledge described as elements involved in growing; author speaks. Existing householder paragraph 2 is separate.
* Coptic 77–78, Baptism/Chrism/Eucharist/Bridal Chamber paragraph 15: fragrant ointment and an application of the Samaritan's wine/oil; narrator interprets a detail of Luke 10:30–37 differently, not a full retelling. Paragraph includes other material and modern paragraph is long, so select carefully, maintain context.
* Coptic 81–82, Spiritual Growth paragraph 4: dogs eating crumbs from the table, compare Matthew 15:26–27/Mark 7:27–28; within a longer bridal-chamber comparison, narrator, partial imagery only.
* Coptic 84–85, Uprooting Evil paragraph 5: quotes the unplanted plant being uprooted, compare Matthew 15:13, Thomas 40; already included Philip paragraph 2 is a different long discussion and does not include paragraph 5.
* Coptic 85, Conclusion paragraph 1: lamps and marriage at night, partial imagery with Matthew 25:1–13; gaps in text prevent a more specific match.
* Coptic 55, The Rulers paragraph 2: bread from heaven, compare John 6:32–35, narrator's analogy. Paragraph 3 has truth sown and reaped, thematic seed comparison.
* Coptic 56, The Resurrection paragraph 2: costly treasure concealed in something cheap; narrator's body/soul simile, no claim it is the Matthew treasure story.
* Coptic 63–64, Becoming Christians paragraph 15: name borrowed at interest vs a gift. 60, Father/Son/Spirit paragraph 7: a child's inheritance before and after adulthood. 60–61, Humans and Animals paragraph 1: cultivating with animals as comparison to a perfect human with powers. 69, Adam/Eve paragraph 5: seeing in mirror/water and light, interpreted as baptism and anointing. These are further separate, developed analogies, not direct Gospel accounts.
* 67–68, Overcoming the World paragraph 7: fragmentary words attributed to Lord about inside/outside; compare Thomas 22 and 2 Clement 12 cautiously and preserve all gaps. Source https://www.gospels.net/philip.

## Gospel of Truth

* Coptic 35–36, Restoring What Was Needed paragraph 2: doctor goes where sickness is, author's comparison, Matthew 9:12–13/Mark 2:17/Luke 5:31–32 are similar but have a different application.
* Coptic 36–37, The Father's Paradise paragraph 1: father/planter and his plants, compare Matthew 15:13 or John 15:1–8 only as imagery; no direct quotation asserted.
* Coptic 36, Restoring paragraph 3: full jars sealed/anointed and leaking jars; compare its own already selected jars passage only, distinguish this image.
* Coptic 21–22, Book of the Living paragraph 4: awakening from drunkenness. Coptic 22–23 paragraph 6: letters as living knowing beings. Coptic 24–25, Return to Unity paragraphs 3–4: darkness dissolving when light comes. These are authorial analogies, not direct Jesus stories. Source https://www.gospels.net/truth.

## Shepherd of Hermas

* Vision 3: current tower entry ends at `vision-3` paragraph 21, before its own explanation at paragraphs 22–43 and continuation `vision-3-part-2` paragraph 1. Include or offer the explanation of the various stones; same vision, not a new story. Its 7 women supporting tower and interpretation follow in part-2 paragraphs 3–10; optional separate image.
* Vision 3 part 2 paragraphs 33–43: the woman's successive forms and the explicit simile of an older man unexpectedly receiving an inheritance; the young man supplies the interpretation. Not a Gospel parable.
* Vision 4 paragraphs 5–22: the beast and four colors, interpreted in the work as tribulation; symbolic vision, not Jesus speaking. Include if visions are within room scope.
* Mandate 6 paragraphs 1–4: straight and crooked paths, compare the two ways in Didache and Matthew 7:13–14/Luke 13:24 only with differences.
* Mandate 10 paragraphs 3–6: neglected vineyard choked by thorns; shepherd compares distractions to weeds, partial to the sower's thorns Matthew 13:22/Mark 4:18–19/Luke 8:14. Paragraph 15 vinegar in wine is a second mixed-substances analogy; existing wormwood/honey is mandate 5:1–6, not this passage.
* Mandate 11 paragraphs 13–16: empty vessels in a storeroom, an image for false prophets; currently selected paragraphs 18–21 are stone and water. Partial to Gospel of Truth's jars only.
* Mandate 12 paragraphs 23–25: full and empty jars of wine, the shepherd's simile about temptation; distinct from Truth's jars and mandate 11.
* `another-parable-part-2` paragraphs 4–16: shepherd explains the vineyard servant parable. Existing `another-parable` paragraphs 27–37 give the story but omit this interpretation. Add as contextual continuation if room intends explanations.
* Vision 1 paragraph 15 smith hammering; mandate 5 paragraphs 7–14 evil and Holy Spirits crowd a single vessel (already 1–6 contains the first presentation of this theme). These are narrower possible additions.
* Existing Parables 6 (two shepherds), 8 (willow), 9 (tower) and `parables-which-he-spake-with-me` all selected fully by section; `another-parable` 1–21 covers elm/vine and seasonal trees. Parable 7 is discussion rather than a separate narrated comparison; 10 returns to tower and personified virgins. Original source https://www.earlychristianwritings.com/text/shepherd-lightfoot.html.

## Epistula Apostolorum

* Chapter 49, paragraph 2: grain gathered and chaff burned as judgment, spoken by the work's risen Jesus. Compare Matthew 3:12/Luke 3:17, where John the Baptist speaks; different narrative setting and speaker. James's translation preserves Ethiopic/Coptic distinctions and gaps; do not erase them.
* Chapter 24: direct/narrow way, compare Matthew 7:13–14/Luke 13:24 and Hermas Mandate 6 with differences. Chapter 31: Saul likened to chosen vessel and a wall that cannot be overturned; distinct images in Jesus's reported speech, also retain witness notes.

## Eusebius

The only Eusebius item in the room is the specifically bounded Theophany 4.22 variant about the three servants. Theophany 4.15–18, 4.28–29 and 4.33–34 quote or discuss Gospel parables already in the room; e.g. 4.33 sower, 4.34 wheat/weeds, with explanation. These are Eusebius's quotations/commentary on canonical stories, not new noncanonical stories; decide whether the room wants reception quotations before adding them. Source https://www.tertullian.org/fathers/eusebius_theophania_05book4.htm. The other four books of Theophany have not been audited section by section; allusions, interpretations and its many original analogies remain an open scope, and book 4 itself has only been searched around parables, not reviewed in full.

## Caveats for implementation

The site has two editions of several patristic works, and the earlier audit found transcription errors in the CCEL-based Lightfoot records. Compare proposed snippets with the named original scan or use a verified historical edition, and preserve uncertainty and source provenance. Existing excerpt hashes pin the received bytes only; they are not evidence of source accuracy. Do not conflate direct parallels with recurring motifs; label early works and distinguish narrator, characters, quoted Jesus and John the Baptist. Gospel and source-coverage candidates here are findings for a follow-up, not implemented or independently reviewed.

Additional Thomas figures worth considering under the same broad boundary: 19 (five enduring trees, image interpretation uncertain), 25 (protect a brother like the eye's pupil), 56 and 80 (world/body or corpse), 62 (left hand/right hand, compare Matthew 6:1–4), 70 (bringing forth what is within), 77 (light and split wood/lifted stone), 95 (lending and expecting nothing back, compare Luke 6:34–35 with different emphasis), 108 (drinking from Jesus's mouth), and 113 (kingdom spread over earth but unseen, partial to Luke 17:20–21). These are short sayings or analogies rather than developed narrative parables; no forced historical dependence. 84, 87, 101 and 112 may also qualify under a very broad standard; they were not independently reviewed here.

Eusebius further finding, 1.1–2: the writer compares a world needing governance with a house needing care, a ship a shipwright and a city an architect; 1.2 compares admiring a building but ignoring its architect. This is the author's apologetic analogy, not a story or saying attributed to Jesus. It is a candidate if the room includes original analogies from the whole Theophany, and illustrates why restricting the audit to the existing 4.22 excerpt cannot establish whole-work coverage. The public-domain 1843 Lee translation is https://www.tertullian.org/fathers/eusebius_theophania_02book1.htm.
