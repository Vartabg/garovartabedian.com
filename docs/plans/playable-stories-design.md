# Playing beside the text

## Accepted for this edition

A reader can play as Jesus in the Jerusalem temple court or take wing over Mount Hermon, then open the texts behind the scene. The existing Stories shelf keeps its 27 illustrated readings. Playable stories have their own index beside it.

For *The overturned tables*, Garo rejected the three-turn witness game. In that version, the player chose a viewpoint or made room for a companion. He asked to walk through the court as Jesus, overturn its furnishings and play through the rest of the account.

Mark describes Jesus overturning the money changers’ tables and dove sellers’ seats, stopping people from carrying things through the temple, and teaching about a house of prayer for all nations. John describes him spilling the changers’ money. Players can choose the order in which they overturn the furnishings and bar the passage. The teaching appears when they finish. The five furnishings, east passage, order of play and movements of the merchants and birds belong to the adaptation. [Mark 11:15–18, World English Bible](https://garovartabedian.com/scriptures/protestant/MRK/11?t=web#v15) · [John 2:13–17, World English Bible](https://garovartabedian.com/scriptures/protestant/JHN/2?t=web#v13)

The reading also links to El Greco’s later painting of the scene. [National Gallery of Art painting record](https://www.nga.gov/artworks/43723-christ-cleansing-temple)

For **Mount Hermon**, the accepted setting is the summit at dawn in the days of Jared. The early Jewish Book of the Watchers, received in the Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo canon as part of 1 Enoch, tells of the Watchers’ descent and oath. The game imagines the light and landscape, the winged protector and the encounters. Free flight lets someone explore the mountain. In *The Cloven Arch*, the player reads the approaching Watcher and chooses a flight line. That choice determines whether the shepherds carry their coal to a signal fire. The shepherds, arch, pursuit and signal belong to the game. [1 Enoch 6, R. H. Charles translation](https://garovartabedian.com/scriptures/ethiopian/ENO/6?t=charles#v4)

## The Valley of Elah

The site already tells David’s story through illustrated stops and links to 1 Samuel. A Valley of Elah **game is a proposal**. Its opening would let a player feel the weight of Saul’s unfamiliar armor and the exposure of setting it aside. A sustained challenge would give way to one abstract sling action and the sudden movement of the two armies. Include an untimed choice so the turning point is available with a tap or a deliberate keypress. The text supplies David putting off the armor and the outcome of his encounter with Goliath. The timing, controls and sensations would be inventions. [Illustrated David reading](https://garovartabedian.com/scriptures/stories/david) · [1 Samuel 17:38–52, World English Bible](https://garovartabedian.com/scriptures/protestant/1SA/17?t=web#v38)

## What remains open

The horizontal arcade view is a design assumption for the courtyard playtest. It gives the player room to walk along the colonnade and move deeper into the court. The final perspective and art direction remain open. Watch whether a first-time player can reach and overturn a table, see the coins scatter and choose where to go next. The player’s response will tell us whether the action is worth repeating.

Garo also reopened two longer-term possibilities. In a continuous turn-based adventure, the player would stay with recurring characters and learn one system over many encounters. In an anthology, the player would have a different role and rhythm in each episode. A shared illustrated book could hold the episodes and source readings. The format and number of episodes remain open.

The earlier stance-duel and dive experiments established a two-action encounter with a readable cue. Interest in a full game built on that encounter remains untested. The flooded city, drones and ash-era sanctuary were retired from Hermon.
